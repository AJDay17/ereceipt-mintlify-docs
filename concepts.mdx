eReceipt connects three things: a **customer**, the **payment card** they linked, and the **merchant** where that card was used. When a payment on a linked card is processed at a participating merchant, eReceipt matches the transaction and delivers an itemized digital **receipt** into the customer's mobile app.

Understanding the objects below will help you talk about the platform accurately whether you're onboarding customers or supporting merchants.

## Customer

A customer is an individual using the eReceipt mobile app. Customers sign up with an email, link one or more payment cards, and receive digital receipts automatically from any participating merchant they pay.

Customers can:

- View every itemized receipt in a single feed.
- Search receipts by merchant, date, or item.
- Tag receipts for expense reporting and export them.
- Browse coupons and deals from nearby merchants.
- Save favorite merchants and refer friends.

## Linked card

A linked card is the credit or debit card a customer has connected to their eReceipt account. Card linking is powered by **Plaid** — customers authenticate with their bank, and eReceipt receives a secure token used only to match transactions. Card numbers and login credentials are never stored by eReceipt.

Card linking is what makes receipt capture automatic. Without a linked card, the cashier would need to ask for an email or phone number the same way they do today.

## Merchant

A merchant is a business that has enabled eReceipt at checkout. When a linked card is used at a merchant location, eReceipt matches the transaction to that merchant and creates a receipt.

Merchants can also have multiple **branches** (physical locations). Each receipt records which specific branch produced it, so both customers and merchants can see exactly where a purchase happened.

## Receipt

A receipt is the itemized record of a single transaction. Every receipt includes:

- Merchant name, branch, and location.
- Date and time of the transaction.
- Line items with quantity, unit price, and tax.
- Subtotal, tax, and total.
- Payment method used (card type and last four digits).
- Any authorization or transaction identifiers passed from the merchant's POS.

Receipts appear in the customer's app as soon as the transaction is processed and matched.

## Coupon

Coupons are deals published by merchants directly inside the eReceipt app. Customers discover them in several ways:

- **Nearby** — deals from merchants around the customer's current location.
- **Affiliate** — deals from partner brands.
- **Favorites** — deals from merchants a customer has saved.
- **Referral** — rewards for inviting new customers.

Merchants can create coupons for free from the merchant tools.

## Expense reporting

Customers who use eReceipt for work can tag receipts, categorize them, and export them for reimbursement. This replaces the common "camera roll full of blurry paper receipts" workflow with a structured export.

## Merchant analytics

Merchants who offer eReceipt at checkout get access to analytics that would be impossible to generate from anonymous card swipes alone, including:

- Total, new, and lost customers over time.
- Retention rates and customer lifetime value.
- Revenue trends and campaign impact.
- Visit and purchase frequency.
- Customer satisfaction scores.

These insights come from real, itemized transaction data — not from surveys or scanned paper.

## How it fits together

<Steps>
  <Step title="Customer links a card">
    In the eReceipt app, the customer connects a payment card via Plaid.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Customer pays at a merchant">
    The customer uses the linked card at a participating merchant. Nothing about the checkout experience changes for the customer or the cashier.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transaction is matched">
    eReceipt matches the transaction to both the customer's linked card and the merchant's branch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receipt appears in the app">
    An itemized receipt is created and delivered to the customer's app feed. The merchant simultaneously gets analytics on that visit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Coupons and reporting close the loop">
    The customer can act on coupons from that merchant, and merchants can use the aggregated insights to guide future campaigns.
  </Step>
</Steps>
